Australian Small Businesses: A Data Story

Small businesses make up over 97% of all Australian enterprises and are a cornerstone of local employment and economic resilience. This page explores where they are concentrated, how they survive, how they're structured, and which sectors they power most — using data from the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS).

1. Where Small Businesses Are Concentrated

Small business counts vary sharply by state, largely tracking population and economic scale. NSW and Victoria together account for the majority of Australia's small business population.

The gap between entries and exits narrowed after the pandemic-era business boom of 2021, suggesting the market is stabilising rather than continuing rapid expansion.

6. 4-Year Survival Rate by Industry

Agriculture businesses show the strongest long-term survival rate, while hospitality (Accommodation & Food) struggles most — consistent with its traditionally thin margins and high turnover.

7. Business Structure Breakdown

The majority of Australian small businesses operate as sole traders, reflecting low barriers to entry for solo and micro enterprises rather than incorporated companies.

8. Small Business Employment Share by Sector

In Construction and Agriculture, small businesses account for the majority of sector employment — these industries rely far more heavily on small operators than large corporates.

9. Survival Rate vs Employment Share

Plotting survival rate against employment share reveals whether sectors that rely more on small business also tend to have more resilient small businesses. Point size reflects total business count in that industry.

10. Entry Trend by State

Faceting the entry trend by state shows growth patterns are not uniform — NSW and VIC drive most of the national trend, while smaller states remain comparatively flat.
